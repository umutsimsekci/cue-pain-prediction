# AI assistance and responsibility

OpenAI Codex (OpenAI; used during preparation in 2026; exact model versions were not retained) assisted with code development and inspection, numerical reproduction, literature organisation, plotting code, manuscript drafting, language editing and submission-package preparation. Assistance was not limited to grammar correction. For the 16 October revision, this included the exploratory cue-mean-only ablation implementation, its computational checks, figure-generating code, a later path-only replay adapter, and revision of the research narrative and accompanying documentation.

Reported numerical results were calculated by executable Python code from public source observations. The research figures were rendered programmatically from these outputs. Source data, original calculations and the documented analysis plan were preserved. The original investigators retain attribution for their experiment and findings. Publication details and numerical statements were checked against source records and reproducible outputs; these computational checks do not constitute independent human scientific validation.

No AI tool is listed as an author. The human author is responsible for reviewing and verifying the text, references, code, figures and interpretations, and for the final content. This record does not assert that final human review has been completed or that a journal submission has been sent or accepted.

## Current publisher guidance

Sage's journal policy distinguishes assistive editing from generative contributions to content, methods, analysis and visualisation. The latter require disclosure. The manuscript's Methods or Acknowledgements and any requested submission declarations should accurately describe the actual uses above rather than describe them as grammar editing alone.
